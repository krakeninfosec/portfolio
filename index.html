About Me

I'm Rehan Fazal, also known as Kraken — a cybersecurity learner from Uttar Pradesh, India, building my skills one experiment, one challenge, and one mistake at a time.

My interest in cybersecurity goes beyond learning tools or following tutorials. I want to understand how systems actually work, where they fail, how vulnerabilities can be exploited, and what it takes to make those systems more secure. I learn by getting hands-on, testing ideas in controlled environments, troubleshooting what goes wrong, and figuring out why something works the way it does.

Right now, I'm exploring the five core pillars of cybersecurity: Offensive Security, Defensive Operations, Security Engineering, Governance, Risk & Compliance (GRC), and Cyber Threat Intelligence & Research. I'm not trying to rush into a specialization before understanding the bigger picture. My goal is to build a solid foundation, explore different sides of the field, and eventually find where my skills and interests can make the most difference.

Most of my learning happens through practical exercises, lab environments, technical research, and independent experimentation. I explore platforms and setups such as Hack The Box Pro Labs, Pentester Academy AttackDefense, RangeForce, DetectionLab, REMnux, FLARE-VM, and Security Onion. Each environment teaches me something different — whether it's understanding an attack path, investigating suspicious activity, analyzing malware, or learning how defenders detect and respond to threats.

I also document what I learn through technical notes, write-ups, small scripts, and personal projects. I believe there's value in documenting failures just as much as successes. When something breaks, I want to understand why instead of simply moving on to the next tutorial.

Alongside my learning, I'm experimenting with a small AI-assisted cybersecurity tool to explore how automation can support security analysis, log investigation, attack-chain understanding, and detection workflows. It's a work in progress, and I'm treating it as an opportunity to learn the engineering behind the idea rather than making claims about what it can do.

I'm still early in my journey, and I have plenty left to learn. This portfolio isn't meant to present me as someone who knows everything. It's a place to document what I'm working on, share what I discover, and show how my skills develop over time.

I'm here to build real skills, understand the details, and earn my progress through hands-on work — not titles.
